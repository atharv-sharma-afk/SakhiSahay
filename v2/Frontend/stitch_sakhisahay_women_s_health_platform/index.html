Understanding the follicular and luteal phases

The menstrual cycle has two halves separated by ovulation. Hormone levels — mainly oestrogen and progesterone — rise and fall across them, which is why energy, mood and symptoms can feel different from week to week.

Follicular phase

Begins on the first day of your period and ends at ovulation. Follicle-stimulating hormone helps an egg mature while oestrogen gradually rises. Many people notice energy returning after the period ends.

Luteal phase

After ovulation, progesterone rises and basal body temperature increases slightly. In the days before the next period some people experience premenstrual symptoms such as bloating, breast tenderness or mood changes.

infoPhase lengths vary from person to person and cycle to cycle. The NHS describes cycles of 21 to 35 days as typical. This is general information, not medical advice.
