Cycle Science

Understanding Follicular vs. Luteal Phase

The menstrual cycle is a rhythmic symphony orchestrated primarily by estrogen and progesterone. Divided into two dominant phases separated by ovulation, understanding your biological state allows for intuitive harmony with work, movement, and emotional rest.

1. The Follicular Phase (Days 1–13)

Beginning with menstruation and continuing until ovulation, follicle-stimulating hormone (FSH) stimulates the growth of ovarian follicles. As estrogen steadily ascends, insulin sensitivity improves, serotonin production increases, and your capacity for creative synthesis and strenuous workouts reaches its monthly zenith.

2. The Luteal Phase (Days 15–28)

Following ovulation, the ruptured follicle transforms into the corpus luteum, producing progesterone. Progesterone exerts a calming, sedative effect on the central nervous system while elevating basal body temperature by ~0.3°C. Metabolic demand rises slightly, prompting higher carbohydrate needs and craving for restorative sleep.

Clinical Takeaway: Honor your follicular surge for bold creative endeavors, and gently transition into restorative pacing as progesterone leads the luteal window.
